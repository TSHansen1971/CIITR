### A2. Lemmata and axiomatic bindings (kernel list)

| Lemma id | Formal binding | Normative consequence |
|---|---|---|
| $\mathrm{A2.1}$ (Axis necessity) | $C_s(S \mid \Pi,\mathcal{O})=1 \Longrightarrow \mathrm{Adm}_\Pi(\Phi_i,S)=1 \wedge \mathrm{Adm}_\Pi(R_g,S)=1$ | Positive classification is admissible only under joint axis admissibility. |
| $\mathrm{A2.2}$ (Non-compensation) | $\mathrm{Adm}_\Pi(\Phi_i,S)\neq 1 \vee \mathrm{Adm}_\Pi(R_g,S)\neq 1 \Longrightarrow C_s(S \mid \Pi,\mathcal{O})\neq 1$ | No axis substitution is admissible, non-admissibility on one axis blocks positivity. |
| $\mathrm{A2.3}$ (Protocol dependence) | $(\Pi_1 \neq \Pi_2) \Longrightarrow \exists S, C_s(S \mid \Pi_1,\mathcal{O}_1) \neq C_s(S \mid \Pi_2,\mathcal{O}_2)$ | CIITR outcomes are protocol-scoped and cannot be asserted as protocol-free system attributes. |
| $\mathrm{A2.4}$ (Disqualification primacy) | $\mathrm{Disq}_\Pi(S)=1 \Longrightarrow C_s(S \mid \Pi,\mathcal{O})\neq 1$ | Disqualifiers have binding authority over positive claims. |
| $\mathrm{A2.5}$ (Decidability gating) | $\mathrm{Decidable}_\Pi(\Phi_i,S)=0 \vee \mathrm{Decidable}_\Pi(R_g,S)=0 \Longrightarrow C_s(S \mid \Pi,\mathcal{O})=\varnothing$ | If admissibility cannot be decided under declared rules, the only admissible output is undefined. |
| $\mathrm{A2.6}$ (Snapshot implication) | $\mathrm{SnapshotOnly}(\Pi)=1 \Longrightarrow \mathrm{Decidable}_\Pi(R_g,S)=0$ | Snapshot-only regimes cannot decide admissibility of the re-entry axis. |
| $\mathrm{A2.7}$ (Temporal invariance nulling) | $\mathrm{TempInv}_\Pi(S)=1 \Longrightarrow \mathrm{Adm}_\Pi(R_g,S)\neq 1$ | Temporal invariance is a null condition for the re-entry axis under the kernel. |
| $\mathrm{A2.8}$ (Constraint gating for certification relevance) | $\mathrm{CertClaim}_\Pi(S)=1 \Longrightarrow \mathrm{Accounted}_\Pi(E_\Pi(S))=1$ | Certification-grade positivity is inadmissible without energy accounting. |
| $\mathrm{A2.9}$ (CPJ definability gating) | $\mathrm{CertClaim}_\Pi(S)=1 \wedge \mathrm{CPJ}_\Pi(S)\neq\varnothing \Longrightarrow \widehat{\Phi}_i(S \mid \Pi)\neq\varnothing \wedge \widehat{R}_g(S \mid \Pi)\neq\varnothing$ | If CPJ is invoked as a gate, its scalar proxies must be declared and defined under the protocol. |
| $\mathrm{A2.10}$ (Illicit ontological return prohibition) | $P(R) \not\Rightarrow P(S)$ | Received and interpreted output does not, by itself, justify claims about the internal nature of the system. |
| $\mathrm{A2.11}$ (Containment) | $P(O) \subseteq P(P)$ | Actual output is one actualization from the potential space. Occurrence does not demonstrate representativeness. |
| $\mathrm{A2.12}$ (Structural derivation) | $P(P) = f(P(S))$ | Potential space is structurally derived from system properties, not arbitrary or observer-created. |
| $\mathrm{A2.13}$ (Reception non-identity) | $P(O) \neq P(R)$ | Emitted output is not identical with received output. Reception is interpretive construction, not transparent access. |
| $\mathrm{A2.14}$ (Cross-attribution prohibition) | $P(R_i) \not\Rightarrow P(S_j)$ for all $i,j$ | No reception on the communication surface licenses ontological claims about any system. |
| $\mathrm{A2.15}$ (Invariance condition) | $F \in \mathrm{Struct}(S) \Longleftrightarrow \forall T \in \mathcal{T}(S), \forall s \in \mathrm{State}(S), F(T(s)) = F(s)$ | Structural properties are defined by invariance under admissible identity-preserving state transformations. |
| $\mathrm{A2.16}$ (Non-invariance sufficiency for denial) | $\exists s \in \mathrm{State}(S), \exists T \in \mathcal{T}(S): F(s) \neq F(T(s)) \Longrightarrow F \notin \mathrm{Struct}(S)$ | A single counterexample under admissible transformation suffices to deny structural status. |
| $\mathrm{A2.17}$ (Simulated-structural inequality) | $(\forall t \in T_{\mathrm{obs}}: F(O_t)) \not\Rightarrow F \in \mathrm{Struct}(S)$ | Behavioural adequacy across an observational domain does not warrant structural attribution. Performance claims quantify over interactions; structural claims quantify over system states. |
| $\mathrm{A2.18}$ (Binary-continuous unification) | $R_g = 0 \Longrightarrow C_s = \Phi_i \times R_g = 0$ | The null-point of the multiplicative composition is the formal expression of the binary denial. The binary threshold and the continuous metric operate at different logical levels. |

### A5. Admissibility conditions for back-inference (kernel reference, from Section 2.7)

Back-inference from communicative event to system condition is warranted only under the following four conditions, each derivable from the invariance principle:

| Condition | Requirement | Invariance-principle derivation |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Systematic field-mapping | The relevant possibility field $P(P)$ SHALL be mapped across varied conditions sufficient to expose the structure of the system's actual possibility envelope. | Expands the transformation domain $\mathcal{T}(S)$ under which invariance is tested. |
| 2. Inclusion of the unrealised | The mapping SHALL identify what the system systematically did not produce under conditions where the relevant structural property would have made such production available or stable. | Attends to states $s$ where $F(T(s))=0$. |
| 3. Progressive explanation compression | Alternative structural explanations SHALL be sufficiently narrowed by the profile of realization and non-realization. | Reduces the set of structural hypotheses compatible with the observed invariance profile. |
| 4. Constraint-profile targeting | The inference SHALL target what the system cannot sustain before positively specifying what it is. | Exploits the intrinsic asymmetry: non-invariance under a single transformation suffices for denial; confirmation requires invariance across the full class. |
