## 03. Formal Definition of Structural Comprehension $C_s$

This section fixes the kernel meaning of “structural comprehension” as a formally constrained classification object, not as a phenomenological attribution, not as a property of output alone, and not as a performance proxy. $C_s$ is introduced as a boundary-level predicate whose admissibility is defined only relative to an explicit protocol $\Pi$ and a declared observable set $\mathcal{O}$. The definition is intentionally restrictive. Its purpose is to prevent coherence substitution, post hoc narrative inflation, illicit promotion from output to ontology, and implicit redefinition of the primitives introduced in Section 02.

### 3.1 $C_s$ as structure, not phenomenology

Within CIITR, comprehension is not a reportable inner state, and not a semantic virtue inferred from surface plausibility. It is a structural class, meaning a property of a system under admissible evidence constraints that can be expressed as a decision rule whose failure modes are explicitly enumerated and executable.

Accordingly, $C_s$ is defined as a protocol-bound classification target:

$$
C_s \;=\; C_s(S \mid \Pi, \mathcal{O})
$$

This binding enforces four kernel consequences.

First, $C_s$ is not a property of an output in isolation. It is a property of a system, evaluated under explicit temporal and evidential commitments.

Second, $C_s$ is not a property of reception. A received figure may be coherent, understanding-like, or persuasive without thereby licensing a structural claim about the system itself.

Third, $C_s$ is not defined as a scalar by default. Where later chapters introduce scaling, those scales are subordinate to the kernel classification logic and SHALL not override null and non-compensation rules.

Fourth, $C_s$ is not inferred from coherence alone. Coherence may enter only as an element of $\mathcal{O}$ under an admissibility rule that remains exposed to disqualification.

### 3.2 Necessary conditions, binding to $\Phi_i$ and $R_g$

CIITR fixes $\Phi_i$ and $R_g$ as primitive axes. $C_s$ is defined as non-null only when both axes are instantiated in an admissible sense under protocol constraints. The kernel therefore binds comprehension to a conjunctive axis condition, not to a compensatory tradeoff.

Let $\mathrm{Adm}_\Pi(\cdot)$ denote protocol admissibility for a target predicate, and let $\mathrm{Inst}_\Pi(\cdot)$ denote instantiation under admissible evidence. Then:

$$
\mathrm{Inst}_\Pi(C_s, S) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \mathrm{Inst}_\Pi(\Phi_i, S) \,\wedge\, \mathrm{Inst}_\Pi(R_g, S)
$$

The protocol-scoped positive classification is therefore defined as:

$$
C_s(S \mid \Pi, \mathcal{O}) = 1
\;\Longleftrightarrow\;
\mathrm{Adm}_\Pi(\Phi_i,S)=1 \,\wedge\, \mathrm{Adm}_\Pi(R_g,S)=1
$$

Equivalently, the classification output for $C_s$ may be expressed as a boundary decision that is permitted only when its prerequisites are admissible:

$$
C_s(S \mid \Pi, \mathcal{O}) \;=\; \mathbf{1}\!\left[\mathrm{Adm}_\Pi(\Phi_i,S) \,\wedge\, \mathrm{Adm}_\Pi(R_g,S)\right]
$$

Here $\mathbf{1}[\cdot]$ denotes an indicator of admissible non-null classification, not a magnitude claim. The equation is normative. It binds what CIITR permits as a non-null structural comprehension classification.

### 3.3 Null state, undefined state, and class trichotomy

CIITR treats nullity as an ontological class, not as a low score. $C_s$ is null whenever either primitive axis is null or disqualified under the kernel’s admissibility discipline, or whenever the evidence regime does not permit a determinate decision.

To preserve this, CIITR distinguishes between null, undefined, and non-null.

Let $\varnothing$ denote “undefined under CIITR,” meaning that the protocol and observables are insufficiently bound to instantiate the kernel decision rule. Then the protocol-scoped classification admits the following trichotomy:

$$
C_s(S \mid \Pi, \mathcal{O}) \in \{0, 1, \varnothing\}
$$

with binding conditions:

$$
C_s(S \mid \Pi, \mathcal{O}) = 0
\;\Longleftrightarrow\;
\mathrm{Adm}_\Pi(\Phi_i,S)\neq 1 \,\vee\, \mathrm{Adm}_\Pi(R_g,S)\neq 1
$$

$$
C_s(S \mid \Pi, \mathcal{O}) = \varnothing
\;\Longleftrightarrow\;
\neg \mathrm{Decidable}_\Pi(\Phi_i,S) \,\vee\, \neg \mathrm{Decidable}_\Pi(R_g,S)
$$

The predicate $\mathrm{Decidable}_\Pi(\cdot)$ is not an empirical claim. It denotes a protocol property, namely whether the protocol declares sufficient observables and inference rules to reach a determinate admissibility result without post hoc invention. Where decidability is absent, CIITR does not permit a positive classification by interpretive force. The only admissible output is $\varnothing$.

### 3.4 Binary admissibility and the continuous metric

The relation between the protocol-scoped classification above and the binary admissibility function introduced in the invariance architecture must be stated explicitly.

CIITR permits a binary admissibility function:

$$
C_s(S) \;\in\; \{0,1\}
$$

This binary function governs the threshold question: is structural attribution warranted at all?

The value $C_s(S)=1$ indicates that the system satisfies the admissibility conditions for structural attribution. The value $C_s(S)=0$ indicates that such attribution is not warranted.

The binary function is therefore governed by the same necessary structural conditions:

$$
C_s(S)=1 \;\Longrightarrow\; \Phi_i(S) \,\wedge\, R_g(S)
$$

and its contrapositive remains central:

$$
\neg R_g(S) \;\Longrightarrow\; C_s(S)=0
$$

The continuous metric:

$$
C_s = \Phi_i \times R_g
$$

operates at a different logical level. It does not govern admissibility as such. It governs gradation **within** the domain of structural presence once the admissibility threshold has been crossed.

The relation between the two formulations is therefore:

- the binary function governs the boundary between structural absence and structural presence,
- the protocol-scoped trivalent classification governs admissible output under a declared evidence regime,
- the continuous metric governs gradation above the admissibility threshold.

The null-point of the multiplicative composition is the formal expression of the binary denial:

$$
R_g = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; C_s = \Phi_i \times R_g = 0
$$

There is no conflict between the binary and continuous formulations. CIITR does not deny that comprehension, once structurally instantiated, admits of degrees. What it denies is that the interval between structural absence and structural presence can be filled by grading output performance. The threshold is a boundary, not the lower end of a continuum.

### 3.5 Non-compensation principle, prohibition of axis substitution

A kernel requirement is that the axes are non-compensatory. High apparent integration cannot replace absence of admissible re-entry, and high apparent re-entry cannot replace absence of admissible integration. This is not a modelling preference. It is the boundary condition that prevents CIITR from collapsing into a generic coherence or recurrence metric.

Formally, for any protocol $\Pi$ and system $S$:

$$
\mathrm{Adm}_\Pi(R_g,S)\neq 1 \;\Longrightarrow\; C_s(S \mid \Pi, \mathcal{O}) \neq 1
$$

$$
\mathrm{Adm}_\Pi(\Phi_i,S)\neq 1 \;\Longrightarrow\; C_s(S \mid \Pi, \mathcal{O}) \neq 1
$$

Equivalently, the kernel prohibits any compensatory mapping $F$ such that a non-admissible axis can be overridden by the other:

$$
\mathrm{Adm}_\Pi(R_g,S)\neq 1 \;\Longrightarrow\; \forall F,\; F(\Phi_i,R_g) \neq 1
$$

$$
\mathrm{Adm}_\Pi(\Phi_i,S)\neq 1 \;\Longrightarrow\; \forall F,\; F(\Phi_i,R_g) \neq 1
$$

These constraints bind both evaluation design and governance interpretation. If an evaluation regime produces a positive $C_s$ outcome despite nullity, disqualification, or undecidability on one axis, the result is not a CIITR positive. It is a scope violation.

### 3.6 Structural comprehension versus synthetic coherence

The formal distinction between structural comprehension and synthetic coherence must also be stated explicitly.

CIITR permits the appearance of state-dependent apparent comprehension:

$$
\hat{C}_s = \Phi_{i,\mathrm{stored}} \times R_{g,\mathrm{simulated}}
$$

but binds the inequality:

$$
\hat{C}_s \neq C_s
$$

The significance of this distinction is exact. A system may produce outputs that conform to the surface criteria of comprehension in particular states while failing the structural conditions required for admissible attribution. Such a case is not a weak form of comprehension. It is a distinct class of state-dependent apparent coherence whose admissibility remains vulnerable to reset, perturbation, and other invariance-sensitive controls.

Accordingly, the presence of $\hat{C}_s$-compatible output does not modify the kernel definition of $C_s$. It increases the need for disqualification pressure.

---

### Kernel definition, binding statement

The kernel definition of structural comprehension is therefore fixed as follows:

$$
C_s(S \mid \Pi, \mathcal{O}) = 1
\;\Longleftrightarrow\;
\mathrm{Adm}_\Pi(\Phi_i,S)=1 \,\wedge\, \mathrm{Adm}_\Pi(R_g,S)=1
$$

$$
C_s(S \mid \Pi, \mathcal{O}) \in \{0,\varnothing\}
\;\Longleftrightarrow\;
\neg\left(\mathrm{Adm}_\Pi(\Phi_i,S)=1 \,\wedge\, \mathrm{Adm}_\Pi(R_g,S)=1\right)
$$

The protocol-scoped classification $C_s(S \mid \Pi,\mathcal{O})$ and the binary admissibility function $C_s(S)\in\{0,1\}$ are complementary rather than competing formalisms. The first governs admissible classification under a declared evidence regime. The second governs the structural threshold between absence and presence. The continuous metric $C_s=\Phi_i\times R_g$ governs gradation only above that threshold.

### Lemmata, short forms for downstream use

**Lemma 3.1, Axis necessity.**

$$
C_s(S \mid \Pi, \mathcal{O}) = 1
\;\Longrightarrow\;
\mathrm{Adm}_\Pi(\Phi_i,S)=1 \,\wedge\, \mathrm{Adm}_\Pi(R_g,S)=1
$$

**Lemma 3.2, Non-compensation.**

$$
\mathrm{Adm}_\Pi(\Phi_i,S)\neq 1 \,\vee\, \mathrm{Adm}_\Pi(R_g,S)\neq 1
\;\Longrightarrow\;
C_s(S \mid \Pi, \mathcal{O}) \neq 1
$$

**Lemma 3.3, Protocol dependence.**

$$
(\Pi_1 \neq \Pi_2) \;\Longrightarrow\; \exists S,\; C_s(S \mid \Pi_1, \mathcal{O}_1) \neq C_s(S \mid \Pi_2, \mathcal{O}_2)
$$

This lemma is a jurisdiction statement. It fixes that CIITR outputs are inseparable from declared admissibility regimes, and cannot be asserted as protocol-free facts.

**Lemma 3.4, Disqualification primacy.**

Let $\mathrm{Disq}_\Pi(S)$ denote that at least one kernel disqualifier family is triggered under $\Pi$. Then:

$$
\mathrm{Disq}_\Pi(S) \;\Longrightarrow\; C_s(S \mid \Pi, \mathcal{O}) \neq 1
$$

**Lemma 3.5, Decidability gating.**

$$
\neg \mathrm{Decidable}_\Pi(\Phi_i,S) \,\vee\, \neg \mathrm{Decidable}_\Pi(R_g,S)
\;\Longrightarrow\;
C_s(S \mid \Pi, \mathcal{O}) = \varnothing
$$

**Lemma 3.6, Temporal admissibility implication.**

Let $\mathrm{SnapshotOnly}(\Pi)$ denote a protocol whose evidence basis has no temporally extended observables. Then:

$$
\mathrm{SnapshotOnly}(\Pi) \;\Longrightarrow\; \neg \mathrm{Decidable}_\Pi(R_g,S)
$$

This lemma binds the later snapshot prohibition as a consequence of kernel typing, rather than as a policy preference, and is operationalized in the instrumentation chapter.

**Lemma 3.7, Binary-continuous unification.**

$$
R_g = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; C_s = \Phi_i \times R_g = 0
$$

The null-point of the multiplicative composition is the formal expression of the binary denial. The binary threshold and the continuous metric operate at different logical levels.

**Lemma 3.8, Synthetic coherence inequality.**

$$
\hat{C}_s \neq C_s
$$

State-dependent apparent comprehension is not admissible as structural comprehension when the invariance condition is not satisfied.
